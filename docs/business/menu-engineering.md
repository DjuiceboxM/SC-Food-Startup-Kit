# What Is Menu Engineering?

Menu engineering is the process of designing your menu to maximize:

- Profit
- Speed of service
- Consistency
- Customer satisfaction
- Inventory efficiency

A great menu doesn't simply sell food.

A great menu sells the *right food.*

---

# The Golden Rule

Most food truck failures are not caused by bad food.

They are caused by:

- Too many menu items
- Excess inventory
- Slow ticket times
- Inconsistent preparation
- Low-margin products

The best food truck menu is usually:

- Small
- Fast
- Profitable
- Repeatable

---

# The 80/20 Rule

In many food operations:

- 20% of menu items generate 80% of sales.
- 20% of menu items generate 80% of profit.

Your objective is to identify:

## Winners

The items that:

- Sell frequently
- Make good profit

and prominently feature them.

# Menu Complexity Score

Rate every item:

| Question | Score |
|-----------|-------|
| Unique Ingredient Required | 1 Point |
| Extra Prep Needed | 1 Point |
| Special Equipment Required | 1 Point |
| Takes Over 5 Minutes to Produce | 1 Point |
| High Waste Potential | 1 Point |

## Scoring

### 0-1 Points

Keep it.

### 2-3 Points

Review carefully.

### 4-5 Points

Strong candidate for removal.

# Food Cost Calculation

## Formula

```text
Food Cost % =
Food Cost ÷ Selling Price × 100
```

Example:

```text
Burger Ingredients = $2.75

Selling Price = $12.00

Food Cost % =
2.75 ÷ 12 × 100

= 22.9%
```

Excellent.

---

| Type | Target |
|--------|--------|

| Catering | 25%-40% |
| Beverage | 10%-25% |
| Desserts | 15%-30% |

# Contribution Margin

This is often more important than food cost percentage.

## Formula

```text
Contribution Margin =
Selling Price - Food Cost
```

Example:

```text
Sandwich Price = $12

Food Cost = $3

Contribution Margin = $9
```

Every sandwich sold contributes:

```text
$9
```

toward:

- Payroll
- Fuel
- Insurance
- Commissary Fees
- Profit

# Menu Engineering Matrix

Every product falls into one of four categories.

## Stars

High Profit
High Popularity

Example:

```text
Smashburger
```

Action:

- Feature prominently
- Market heavily
- Never remove

---

## Plow Horses

Low Profit
High Popularity

Example:

```text
French Fries
```

Action:

- Slight price increase
- Reduce portion size carefully
- Bundle in combo meals

---

## Puzzles

High Profit
Low Popularity

Example:

```text
Specialty Loaded Fries
```

Action:

- Better menu placement
- Better photos
- Better description

---

## Dogs

Low Profit
Low Popularity

Example:

```text
Random Fish Sandwich
```

Action:

- Consider removing

# The Ticket Time Rule

Every menu item should answer:

```text
Can this be served in under 4 minutes?
```

For food trucks:

### Under 2 Minutes

Excellent

### 2-4 Minutes

Acceptable

### Over 5 Minutes

Usually problematic

Long ticket times mean:

- Longer lines
- Fewer orders
- Less revenue

# Combo Engineering

Customers love combos.

Example:

```text
Pulled Pork Sandwich
+ Chips
+ Drink
```

Individual Prices:

```text
$12 + $2 + $3 = $17
```

Combo:

```text
$16
```

Customer thinks:

"Saving $1."

Owner thinks:

"Higher average transaction."

Everybody wins.

# Cross Utilization

Never buy an ingredient for only one menu item.

Bad:

```text
Special imported sauce
used only on one sandwich
```

Good:

```text
House sauce
used on:
- Sandwiches
- Fries
- Wraps
- Wings
```

Every ingredient should work multiple jobs.

# New Menu Item Evaluation

Before adding a new item ask:

## Does it use current ingredients?

YES / NO

## Does it require new equipment?

YES / NO

## Does it slow production?

YES / NO

## Does it improve profits?

YES / NO

## Does it fit the brand?

YES / NO

If more than two answers are NO:

Consider rejecting the item.
